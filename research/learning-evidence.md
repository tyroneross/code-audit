# Evidence behind review and incident learning

Checked October 17, 2026. These sources inform the [review contract](../skills/code-principles/references/review-feedback.md) and [incident-learning contract](../skills/code-principles/references/incident-learning.md). They do not certify the plugin, current models or a universal review policy.

| Evidence | Supported lesson | Boundary and implementation decision |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic, [How the Claude Code team does code review](https://claude.dev/blog/solving-code-review-at-anthropic/), October 15, 2026 | Review convergence, clean-case evaluation, project-specific instructions, risk-proportionate attention, staged recovery and outcome feedback matter. | Vendor operational account; source statistics were not independently corroborated. Adopt convergence/evaluation discipline, not default voting, autonomous merging, nightly scans or a universal round cap. Two model reviews of one source are independent judgments, not corroboration of its measurements. |
| Chen et al., [Teaching Large Language Models to Self-Debug](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05128), 2023 | Code explanation and execution results helped repair code in the studied benchmarks. | Earlier models and bounded tasks; not evidence that every extra review round helps. Ask for a discriminating prediction and actual result. |
| Huang et al., [Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798), ICLR 2024 | Intrinsic reconsideration without external feedback could fail or degrade answers on the studied reasoning tasks. | Different tasks from execution-grounded debugging; not a claim that present models cannot correct code. Seek new evidence instead of blind repeated critique. |
| Liu et al., [EvalPlus](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/43e9d647ccd3e4b7b5baab53f0368686-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2023 | Expanded valid tests exposed incorrect generated programs missed by original suites. | Benchmark-specific results. Use contract-relevant boundary, differential and adverse-order tests; also review whether the oracle itself matches the contract. |
| Liu et al., [Lost in the Middle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172), TACL | Relevant-information position affected the studied long-context retrieval/QA tasks. | Not a direct comparison of our prompt formats or current coding models. Keep routing compact and details conditional, and measure local outcomes rather than asserting a format advantage. |

## Local observations and what they do not establish

The original workspace retained three frozen instruction experiments before this combined repository was created. Their full transcripts and private project records are not distributed here. The following summaries preserve limitations; they are historical evidence, not fresh measurements of the current instructions.

- The short prose/pseudocode placement pilot produced passing repairs in each arm. The longer comparison also reached the behavioral ceiling across its runs, as did the timeout follow-up. These trials did not distinguish correctness by format/placement, measure an independent benefit from the shared guidance, or establish broad model equivalence. There was no matched short-versus-long control. Keep the opening pseudocode as a reading aid; do not claim it improves accuracy or that more detail is always better.
- In the timeout simulator, a larger-timeout-only counterfactual still produced duplicate effects and exceeded the overall budget. Adequate repairs preserved operation identity, used one retry owner and respected remaining time. This is a causal example, not a mandate against justified policy constants or proof of real provider idempotency. The separately recorded [Opus repair](../evals/timeout-retry/observed-result.json) and [replay instructions](../evals/README.md) expose the public reproducible boundary.
- Independent oracle review found assertions requiring every allowed retry and another transport send even when a valid cached receipt was available. Both exceeded the written contract. Preserve oracles and changes, and reapply the corrected contract fairly; do not force one implementation or weaken legitimate behavior to rescue a candidate.
- Earlier scanner/review comparisons found valid underlying concerns with incorrectly stretched checklist IDs. Finding validity, rule fit and repair quality need separate adjudication. Candidate counts, function length and a single unreplicated workflow comparison do not establish a winner. Focused search plus causal explanation is preferable to concatenating every guide.
- An August 2026 Build Loop memory ordering test favored relevance over recency on a small lexical query set. It measured ordering, not candidate recall, semantic answer quality or repair success. Candidate filtering must be evaluated separately; a ranker cannot recover excluded records.
- A September 2026 Build Loop memory audit reproduced fallback treating superseded advice as active and mixed telemetry denominators. It did not establish task-time/token savings or a known-but-unused-memory causal failure. Retrieval, inspection, a reported decision effect and verified outcomes remain distinct. Missing receipts do not prove non-use; broad history should not be pruned from low open counts.
- Historical model-profile trials found some second passes useful on particular tasks, while structural omissions remained. Their model rankings and numerical effects are not defaults for current models. Use source-backed dependency/registration checks for missing connections and test any proposed extra pass locally.

## Maintain and test the guidance

The [review evaluation](../evals/review/README.md) contains new synthetic review cases, held-out labels, a baseline and candidate comparison when available, and explicit adjudication. It does not replace task-specific source/runtime checks or establish general defect recall. Re-evaluate meaningful prompt, tool, model or ranking changes on applicable positive and clean controls; distinguish mechanical schema checks from semantic correctness.

For a lesson, retain trigger, context, cause, tried/rejected approaches, adequate action, preserved behavior, verification and limits. Promote repeated confirmed failures into the cheapest adequate check or concise project instruction. Never promote model agreement, retrieval frequency, test count or a consumer-reported effect into proof of benefit. Review evidence must identify the actual revision and environment; stale approvals, configuration or a resolved ticket are not current runtime/delivery proof.
