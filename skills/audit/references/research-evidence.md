# Research behind measurable, contextual code improvement

Checked 2026-10-14. These original papers support methods and expose limits; they do not establish this plugin's effectiveness. Findings below are paraphrases. The operational choices are our engineering synthesis, not rules stated verbatim by every author. Mirrors and different versions of one paper are not independent evidence. Historical model results are not current model rankings.

| ID | Original source and publication | Relevant finding | Transfer limit and plugin decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | Kalibera & Jones, [Rigorous Benchmarking in Reasonable Time](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/33611/), ISMM 2013; corrected author manuscript | Repetition and uncertainty should reflect where variation arises, including iterations, executions and builds. | Author abstract inspected; full PDF retrieval failed. Use independent run units and account for variation; do not count repeated inner-loop timings as independent evidence. |
| R02 | Mytkowicz et al., [Producing Wrong Data Without Doing Anything Obviously Wrong!](https://sape.inf.usi.ch/publications/asplos09.html), ASPLOS 2009 | Seemingly incidental setup differences can bias performance conclusions; the study investigates detection and randomized setups. | Original research-group abstract inspected. Record environment and run ordering. More repeated samples do not fix systematic bias. |
| R03 | Jin et al., [Understanding and Detecting Real-World Performance Bugs](https://bugjin.github.io/pldi-2012-perfbug.pdf), PLDI 2012 | The studied applications included inefficient function combinations, unnecessary work and bugs exposed by workload changes. | Paper's root-cause section inspected. Trace callers and real input conditions; a costly-looking function or pattern alone is not a defect. Historical sampled applications do not establish universal frequencies. |
| R04 | Dean & Barroso, [The Tail at Scale](https://research.google/pubs/the-tail-at-scale/), CACM 2013 | At large service scale and utilization, occasional slow components can dominate responsiveness. | Author abstract inspected. Measure relevant tail latency, queues, throughput and failures at load; do not claim capacity from a low-load median alone or copy distributed techniques into a small local script. |
| R05 | Liu et al., [Is Your Code Generated by ChatGPT Really Correct?](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/43e9d647ccd3e4b7b5baab53f0368686-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2023, EvalPlus | Additional valid tests revealed incorrect code missed by earlier suites. The method combines generated inputs, mutation and contract filtering. | Original paper/testing sections inspected. Preserve correctness separately from performance; use boundary/property/differential tests appropriate to the actual contract. Passing examples does not prove arbitrary behavior. |
| R06 | Du et al., [Mercury](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07844), 2024 author paper | Code-generation correctness and runtime efficiency can diverge; the benchmark evaluates both. | Author abstract inspected; Python task benchmark. Keep separate outcome observations rather than a universal quality score; no claim that its model results transfer to this agent or repository. |
| R07 | Huang et al., [EffiBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02037), 2024 author paper | The benchmark studies execution-time and memory costs of generated solutions against human solutions. | Author abstract inspected; coding-problem population. Measure relevant resource costs alongside runtime; neither a canonical solution nor a leaderboard is a universal production optimum. |
| R08 | He et al., [SWE-Perf](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/he26y.html), ICML 2026; [earlier full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12415v1) | Repository optimization requires executable environments, correctness checks and performance comparisons; the evaluation remeasures the baseline with the candidate. | Publisher abstract and earlier evaluation section inspected; versions may differ. Reproduce the actual baseline and compare required behavior. This benchmark does not certify our plugin or current model. |
| R09 | Peng et al., [Multi-level Code Optimization via Mixture of Prompts](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23665v1), 2026 author version marked ASE 2026 accepted | The method uses differential profiling and strategies at algorithm, statement, expression and API levels; additional optimization effort has costs. | Method and validity sections inspected; Python benchmarks and one base-model setting limit transfer. Prefer bottleneck-specific strategies and bound search cost; do not copy its prompts, numeric threshold or claimed speedups as our defaults. |
| R10 | Chen et al., [Are Performance-Optimization Benchmarks Reliably Measuring Coding Agents?](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01211v1), July 2026 preprint | Cross-machine replay and scoring choices can change benchmark validity and rankings. | Original study/design sections inspected; not treated as settled consensus. Record machine/workload scope and per-task outcomes; a leaderboard rank or marginal timing is insufficient evidence for a local optimization claim. |
| R11 | Becker et al., [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089), July 2025 author study | In this randomized study, perceived productivity and observed task completion time diverged. | Author abstract inspected; experienced developers, mature projects and early-2025 tools. Measure agent/review effort separately from generated-code performance. Do not generalize the study into a claim that current AI always slows or speeds development. |

Current counter-evidence: METR's [February 2026 experiment-design update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/) reports selection and time-measurement problems in its later study. It does not provide a reliable universal current speedup estimate. This update and R11 share an organization and are not independent corroboration.

Implementation choice: reuse the codebase's existing tests, profiler and appropriate benchmark analysis. A custom statistics engine was considered and rejected because it adds maintenance without improving bottleneck discovery or the target code. Research supports careful measurement; it does not require another measurement system for routine fixes.

## What this changes and what remains unknown

Together, the sources support workload-based diagnosis, preserved correctness/resource constraints, comparable measurement, explicit uncertainty and proportional effort. The focused workflow and compact rendering are engineering decisions requiring local checks. No source measures this plugin's finding recall, false positives, real-code optimization gains or general agent accuracy. A small blind trial can expose interpretation issues; broader human-adjudicated evaluations remain necessary before making those claims.
