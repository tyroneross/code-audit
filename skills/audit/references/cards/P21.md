# P21 — Decide what happens when one of several parallel tasks fails

When several tasks form one operation, define whether failure cancels the rest, results are gathered, or work continues under another owner, and report completion according to that contract.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Honor the logical operation's concurrent completion contract.

A composition policy specifies cancellation, joining, ownership transfer, and which failures remain diagnosable.

## Context

**Source applicability:** Parallel batches, fan-out requests, concurrent imports, or helpers that promise to wait for all work.

**Avoid:** Do not impose wait-for-all if children are deliberately independent durable jobs with transferred ownership and the caller's contract allows that behavior.

**Premises:**
- Several tasks contribute to one logical operation.
- The caller relies on a particular meaning of complete or failed.

## Options and costs

### Cancel and wait for remaining tasks

When: One task's failure makes the overall result unusable.

Action: Cancel the remaining tasks where possible and account for when they actually stop.

Gain: Limits wasted work and prevents tasks from outliving resources they still use.

Cost: Waiting for tasks to stop can delay the final failure response.

### Collect every task's outcome

When: Individual results, partial completion, or complete diagnostics remain useful after a failure.

Action: Wait for the tasks and report their successes, failures, and completed effects.

Gain: Gives an accurate batch result and supports complete cleanup.

Cost: The slowest task can delay the overall response.

### Return while another owner supervises

When: Tasks have independent ownership and the caller's contract allows them to continue.

Action: Transfer responsibility and distinguish job acceptance from eventual completion.

Gain: Lets the caller return while useful work continues.

Cost: Another owner must report late failures and account for continuing effects.

## Examples

Example: An importer gathers item outcomes while retaining ownership until all required workers finish.

Counterexample: Returning the first exception while sibling tasks still reference a caller's released buffer violates a wait-for-all contract.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- A batch changes from all-or-nothing to supported partial completion. Change aggregation, publication, and user reporting together; do not merely stop throwing the first error.
- A child becomes independently owned and no longer uses parent resources. Consider earlier parent completion if the contract explicitly permits it.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- Exercise simultaneous child failures, cancellation races, and failure while arranging completion.
- Confirm no child accesses caller-owned resources after the promised completion point.
