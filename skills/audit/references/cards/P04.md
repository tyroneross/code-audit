# P04 — Match design and testing effort to failure risk

Match engineering effort to the cost of failure and expected reuse, using the simplest approach that still meets the required correctness, recovery, and compatibility guarantees.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Meet required guarantees with proportionate engineering effort.

Spend effort where it reduces a consequential uncertainty or recurring cost, and preserve the ability to change cheap experiments.

## Context

**Source applicability:** Choosing test depth, abstraction, automation, or release scope under limited time.

**Avoid:** Do not use a deadline, prototype label, or confident code generation as a reason to waive known correctness or recovery requirements.

**Premises:**
- Failure consequences and expected reuse are sufficiently understood to choose a proportionate approach.
- The reduced scope still satisfies the user's required guarantees.

## Options and costs

### Run a limited experiment

When: The purpose is learning and the result can be discarded before others depend on it.

Action: Build the smallest experiment that answers the question and limit its exposure to users and data.

Gain: Produces early feedback with little long-term maintenance commitment.

Cost: The experiment may need substantial changes before it can support ongoing use.

### Build a focused solution

When: The required behavior is known and the change affects a small part of the system.

Action: Implement a simple solution and add safeguards for the failures that can actually occur.

Gain: Meets the current requirement without paying for speculative infrastructure.

Cost: Larger workloads or new requirements may require revisiting the design.

### Invest in stronger guarantees

When: Data loss, widespread disruption, or difficult recovery makes failure expensive.

Action: Add the recovery, isolation, compatibility, and verification needed for those consequences.

Gain: Reduces specific failure risks that a simpler implementation cannot adequately control.

Cost: Increases design time, delivery time, and continuing operating work.

## Examples

Example: Prototype a visual interaction quickly, then test durable selection logic once the interaction is chosen.

Counterexample: A time-limited release still needs duplicate-prevention guarantees if repeating a remote operation would harm users.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- The experiment now serves customers or stores persistent user data. Reassess validation, ownership, compatibility, and recovery before treating it as production infrastructure.
- The component is being discarded and its results are reproducible. Remove unnecessary generalization and long-term maintenance work while preserving truthful output.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- The selected work meets the required outcome.
- Deferred work has an explicit consequence and revisit trigger; limitations are not presented as completed guarantees.

## Research update (2026-10-14)

- Small batches predict delivery performance; a change that takes more than a week to finish and check is too big. Enforce small batches for AI-generated changes. — [DORA, Working in small batches](https://dora.dev/capabilities/working-in-small-batches/)
- One self-contained change per review; keep refactors separate from behavior changes. — [Google Engineering Practices, Small CLs](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/small-cls.html)
