# P28 — Improve performance only where measurements show a real problem

Optimize only when representative measurements show a meaningful resource problem, and keep a change only if it improves the relevant result while preserving required behavior.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Meet the actual resource objective without sacrificing required behavior.

Measurement identifies the limiting resource; comparing designs under the required workload directs effort toward changes that affect the actual objective.

## Context

**Source applicability:** Latency, throughput, memory, contention, or resource use prevents the required outcome or creates a material operating cost.

**Avoid:** Do not remove safety or clarity for an unmeasured hypothetical gain, or assume native/managed language choice determines the result.

**Premises:**
- A performance or resource objective matters to the user or operation.
- The measurement represents the relevant workload and preserves correctness requirements.

## Options and costs

### Keep the clear implementation

When: Representative workloads already meet the required latency, throughput, memory, or cost target.

Action: Retain the readable implementation and a useful measurement baseline.

Gain: Avoids complexity and fragile optimizations with no demonstrated current benefit.

Cost: A different workload may later expose a bottleneck.

### Remove measured expensive work

When: Measurements identify avoidable calls, allocations, repeated computation, or similar waste.

Action: Change that work and measure the effect on the actual end-to-end objective.

Gain: Targets a known cost and lets the result be checked.

Cost: Caching, batching, or reordered work can change freshness, ordering, or failure behavior.

### Change the underlying design

When: Local improvements cannot meet a demonstrated resource requirement.

Action: Reconsider data structures, work ownership, or deployment while preserving required behavior.

Gain: Can remove a bottleneck caused by the structure of the system.

Cost: Migration adds risk and may shift resource use or complexity to another part of the system.

## Examples

Example: Eliminate repeated remote requests when they dominate latency before tuning a small local loop.

Counterexample: A historical whole-system performance result does not establish that rewriting an application in another language will make its user workflow faster.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- Workload scale or access patterns change. Measure the new bottleneck before continuing the old optimization strategy.
- An optimization no longer materially affects the objective. Consider removing its maintenance cost while preserving a useful performance baseline.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- The target outcome improves under a relevant workload.
- Required semantics, failure behavior, and resource bounds remain acceptable.

## Research update (2026-10-14)

- Knuth's full passage continues: do not pass up opportunities in the critical 3%, and an easy 12% gain is never marginal. Use measurement to find critical code; choosing a known-efficient option of equal simplicity is not premature. — [Knuth, Structured Programming with go to Statements, ACM Computing Surveys 1974, p. 268](https://pic.plover.com/knuth-GOTO.pdf)
- Start a performance investigation by checking utilization, saturation and errors for every resource. — [Gregg, The USE Method](https://www.brendangregg.com/usemethod.html)
