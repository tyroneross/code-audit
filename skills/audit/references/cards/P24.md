# P24 — Simulate difficult test conditions and verify assumptions against real systems

Use a fake or mock when controlled dependency behavior makes a meaningful test practical, and check its assumptions against the real dependency when integration behavior matters.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Control difficult tests while retaining accurate dependency assumptions.

A fake or mock at a meaningful boundary controls a difficult condition while complementary real-boundary checks validate the modeled contract.

## Context

**Source applicability:** Testing time, remote failures, expensive I/O, or specific boundary outcomes that are otherwise difficult to reproduce.

**Avoid:** Do not mock every internal collaborator or treat a fake's successful behavior as proof that the actual dependency behaves the same way.

**Premises:**
- Controlling the dependency improves a meaningful test.
- The double's behavior is sufficiently faithful for the asserted contract.

## Options and costs

### Use a focused fake

When: A test needs control over time, a rare error, or an expensive external operation.

Action: Replace that dependency with a small implementation that supplies the required test conditions.

Gain: Makes difficult cases fast and repeatable.

Cost: The fake can behave differently from the real dependency.

### Check the fake against the real contract

When: Controlled tests are useful and assumptions about the provider may change.

Action: Use the fake for controlled cases and separately verify the relevant behavior of the real adapter.

Gain: Combines repeatable tests with evidence that their dependency model still fits.

Cost: The fake, real adapter, and shared contract checks all need maintenance.

### Use the real local dependency

When: The dependency is inexpensive, predictable, and easy to run in tests.

Action: Use it directly for the tests that depend on its behavior.

Gain: Tests actual behavior and removes a fake that could become inaccurate.

Cost: Tests may take longer, and rare failures can be harder to reproduce.

## Examples

Example: A fake clock exercises expiry behavior while an adapter check verifies the stored timestamp format.

Counterexample: Asserting every private helper call does not establish the user's outcome and can make routine refactoring expensive.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- The provider changes error mapping or retry behavior. Update and revalidate the adapter contract and its doubles together.
- A dependency becomes cheap and deterministic to run locally. Consider using the real component when that reduces modeling complexity without sacrificing useful control.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- A boundary failure can be reproduced deterministically.
- The real adapter matches the assumptions the application test relies on.

## Research update (2026-10-14)

- Prefer real implementations when fast and deterministic, then fakes, then stubs; avoid interaction testing when possible. — [Software Engineering at Google, ch. 13 Test Doubles](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch13.html)
