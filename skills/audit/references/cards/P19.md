# P19 — Retry failed requests only when another attempt can help safely

Retry a failed request only if the next attempt has a reasonable chance of succeeding and can be made without excessive load or harmful duplicate effects.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Improve resilience without unsafe repetition or uncontrolled retry load.

A stable operation identity or otherwise idempotent contract limits duplicate effects; bounded attempts, backoff, and jitter limit retry pressure.

## Context

**Source applicability:** A material operation encounters a retryable environmental failure and all repetition prerequisites are established.

**Avoid:** Do not automatically retry a non-idempotent write, a persistent rejection, an expired idempotency key, or an operation whose outcome is unknown without a reconciliation strategy.

**Premises:**
- The operation is safe to repeat under the actual provider contract.
- The cause is plausibly transient.
- The idempotency scope, payload rules, and retention window cover the intended retry.
- The retry fits the overall deadline and has a deliberate owner.

**Audit clarification:** Code, a proposed change, or a recovery claim decides whether to repeat or reconcile an operation after failure or uncertain completion. Unsafe or uncertain repetition remains relevant. Establish repeat-safety, expected usefulness, load, deadline, ownership, and actual provider guarantees. Missing prerequisites limit or reject repetition; they do not imply not_applicable.

## Options and costs

### Do not repeat yet

When: The failure will persist, or a write may already have completed and repetition could cause harm.

Action: Return the failure, correct the cause, or check the operation's status before deciding to repeat it.

Gain: Avoids harmful duplicate effects and requests that only add load.

Cost: A temporary failure may remain unresolved until a later recovery attempt.

### Retry within limits

When: Another attempt has a reasonable chance of success and the operation is safe to repeat.

Action: Limit attempts and total time; increase delays between attempts and add random variation to avoid synchronized retries.

Gain: Can recover temporary failures without requiring a separate recovery process.

Cost: Adds latency and load; retries can worsen an outage if their limits and delays are poorly chosen.

### Track and reconcile the operation

When: An important remote change must be recovered after a restart or an uncertain response.

Action: Save the operation's identity, determine what completed, and use verified duplicate-prevention guarantees before repeating it.

Gain: Supports recovery without blindly repeating changes that may already have happened.

Cost: Requires durable records and verified rules for duplicate detection, payload matching, and how long those rules remain valid.

## Examples

Example: Retry a timed-out create with the same provider-supported key while its documented guarantee remains valid.

Counterexample: Generating a fresh key on each retry can represent several distinct create intentions rather than one repeated intention.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- The provider adds a documented idempotency guarantee that covers the whole retry interval. Consider bounded automatic retries after validating that contract and the user's latency budget.
- The retention window becomes shorter than possible recovery delays, or key scope changes. Stop relying on the old duplicate-prevention assumption; reconcile first or redesign durable operation tracking.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- Test a timeout before processing and after commitment.
- Test restart, repeated attempts, key expiry or scope boundaries where applicable.
- Confirm the total attempt count and time budget across layers.

## Research update (2026-10-14)

- Retries are one of the most common mechanisms that sustain outages; use a retry budget or disable retries under overload. — [Bronson et al., Metastable Failures in Distributed Systems, HotOS 2021](https://sigops.org/s/conferences/hotos/2021/papers/hotos21-s11-bronson.pdf)
- Retry at one layer only under a server-wide budget: 4 attempts at each of 3 layers sends 64 attempts to the database. — [Google SRE Book, Addressing Cascading Failures](https://sre.google/sre-book/addressing-cascading-failures/)
- Stripe keeps idempotency keys at least 24 hours, rejects reuse with different parameters, and replays the first response. — [Stripe API, Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)
