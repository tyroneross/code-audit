# P05 — Estimate delivery dates from evidence and state the uncertainty

When planning delivery, investigate major unknowns and use comparable completed work where available, stating uncertainty instead of promising precision the evidence cannot support.

Read [audit interpretation](../audit-interpretation.json) first. Review unsafe uses and unsupported claims even when a safe technique prerequisite is absent. Adapt validation examples to the actual contract; combine or stage options when useful.

## Purpose and mechanism

Plan delivery with an honest and relevant evidence basis.

Observed completion history and explicit assumptions constrain estimates and reveal when the comparison basis has changed.

## Context

**Source applicability:** Planning milestones, communicating delivery uncertainty, or deciding whether to reduce scope.

**Avoid:** Do not present a statistical confidence or precise date without a defensible basis; a trivial task need not acquire a scheduling system.

**Premises:**
- There is a real planning decision to inform.
- Past work is comparable enough to be useful, or the lack of comparability is disclosed.

## Options and costs

### Investigate before estimating

When: Unknown requirements or integration behavior prevent a defensible delivery estimate.

Action: Set a limited investigation milestone and identify what must be learned before forecasting delivery.

Gain: Replaces major guesses with evidence.

Cost: A reliable final delivery date remains unavailable until the investigation resolves enough uncertainty.

### Give a range and assumptions

When: Some work is understood, but there is little relevant history or important dependencies remain uncertain.

Action: Give a delivery range and state the dependencies and assumptions that could change it.

Gain: Supports planning without presenting uncertain work as a precise promise.

Cost: The range may be broad and depend heavily on judgment.

### Use comparable delivery history

When: Completed work is sufficiently similar in scope, tools, and integration demands.

Action: Use observed delivery times and distinguish implementation time from integration and release work.

Gain: Bases the forecast on actual variation in comparable work.

Cost: Old timing data can mislead after the team, tools, or kind of work changes.

## Examples

Example: A format migration estimate includes fixture collection and compatibility validation, not only serialization code.

Counterexample: An LLM's confidence in its next answer is not measured delivery history.

## Adapt when assumptions change

- New tooling makes implementation faster but integration work remains similar. Recalibrate those work categories separately rather than applying the implementation speedup to the entire schedule.
- The team, task class, or deployment process changes materially. Reduce reliance on old timing history until new comparable observations exist.

## Context-adapted validation probes

- The estimate covers the intended completion boundary.
- Observed misses update the estimate or its comparison class rather than being explained away.
